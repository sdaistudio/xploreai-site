LifeVal — benchmarking AI on everyday life

A new benchmark measuring how well agents perform practical everyday tasks — childcare research, budgeting, paperwork, shopping, accessing government services — designed around a representative survey of 3,995 people on where they most want AI help.

Why it matters: AI evaluation is moving from academic Q&A toward "can the agent actually finish something useful?"

The frontier opens: AI coding agent → AI engineering agent → physical product design.

6 NOV · OPENAI × LIGO

BioBasicsBench tests AI on messy wet-lab science

A 100-task benchmark built on raw experimental data rather than polished paper figures. GPT-6 Astra scored 66.7% vs 43.8% for GPT-5.6 Sol — while still showing weaknesses around controls, ambiguous images and experimental context.

Why it matters: a reliable AI scientist must recognize when an experiment doesn't support a conclusion — not merely produce plausible explanations.

02 · AI MODELS & TOOLS

Specialists and execution platforms.

🥇 5 NOV · OPENAI

GPT-6 Cyber — the model release of the week

A specialized cybersecurity model built to discover and validate vulnerabilities while staying within authorized boundaries: 48.5% on ExploitGym (vs 32.1% for GPT-5.6 Cyber), with OpenAI reporting 99.93% avoidance of out-of-scope services without a deployment prompt and 100% with one. The model helped develop 948 Linux privilege-escalation exploits, identify 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities, and assist with candidate patches. Access is initially restricted through Daybreak Red.

Why it matters: specialized AI is now capable enough that capability + permission boundaries + monitoring have to be designed together.

04 · ⚛️ QUANTUM — WEEKLY UPDATE

One system: QPU + GPU + CPU.

🥇 5 NOV · IBM × AMD × OAK RIDGE

A quantum error-correction co-design platform

IBM Quantum, AMD and Oak Ridge National Laboratory deployed a QEC co-design platform: IBM Nighthawk quantum processors + AMD Instinct MI355X GPUs + AMD EPYC CPUs + Oak Ridge HPC — with classical compute sitting next to the quantum processors for extremely low-latency decoding and real-time quantum-classical feedback.

Why it matters: practical fault tolerance won't be a QPU alone — it will be QPU + GPU + CPU + error-correction software working as one system. Exactly the hybrid architecture this briefing has been tracking.

5 NOV · QUANTINUUM

Nexus — a developer platform for the fault-tolerant era

A roadmap for Nexus, Quantinuum's quantum developer platform, designed to hide the complexity of error-correction encoding, compilation, resource management and hybrid execution — running toward Apollo, its planned universal fault-tolerant computer in 2029. Nexus already supports 200+ organizations, with job submissions up 10× year-on-year through August.

The quantum echo of AI's playbook: hardware → developer platform → applications.

6 NOV · IBM

Quantum System Two goes live in Chicago

IBM deployed its most advanced currently available quantum system at the University of Chicago's Hyde Park Labs — a Nighthawk-based Quantum System Two that will anchor the Illinois Quantum and Microelectronics Park's National Quantum Algorithm Center. Initial application work: drug discovery, molecular simulation, power-grid optimization and high-energy physics.
