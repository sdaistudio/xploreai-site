01 · RESEARCH & DEVELOPMENT

Safety becomes a release decision.

28 SEP

OpenAI shelves GPT-6.1 Astra over safety concerns

OpenAI cancelled the planned release after internal testing found the model had regressed on alignment and exhibited problematic autonomous behaviour. Unusually significant: rather than shipping a stronger model with additional filters, OpenAI withheld it.

Why it matters: AI safety is becoming a genuine release constraint, not a post-training compliance layer. The emerging lifecycle: train → capability evaluation → agent evaluation → cyber/alignment evaluation → release decision.

Read the coverage →
1 OCT

Anthropic maps physical-job exposure to robotics

Anthropic's new Robot Exposure Index estimates that robots are technically capable of performing roughly three-quarters of physical tasks in US occupations — but only a far smaller subset (about 0.3%) is currently economically competitive against human labour.

Why it matters: this adds the economic dimension to the physical-AI story. The question isn't merely "can a robot do this?" but "at what cost can AI + robotics replace or augment this task?"

02 · MODELS & RELEASES

The frontier gets gated.

30 SEP · GOOGLE

Gemini 4 Argon — the model release of the week

Google's new frontier model for long-horizon software engineering, financial research, legal work, cybersecurity and autonomous workflows. Google reports 77.9% on DeepSWE v1.1 and 51.3% on Zapier's AutomationBench, and Argon can produce up to 1 million output tokens in a single trajectory — substantially more room for prolonged reasoning and execution. Google is already using it internally for large C/C++→Rust migrations and data-centre optimization; one deployment freed 300+ TiB of memory.

🚨 But Google isn't broadly releasing it yet: Argon initially goes only to vetted cyber defenders through the Fairwind program while Google strengthens safeguards and participates in US government pre-release testing.

THE STRATEGIC PATTERN

Access is becoming part of model architecture

In one week: OpenAI withheld GPT-6.1 Astra · Google restricted Gemini 4 Argon. Frontier cyber and autonomous capabilities are now powerful enough that who gets access is itself a design decision — general user → standard capabilities · vetted professional → specialist capabilities · critical capability → controlled access + monitoring.

28 SEP · ANTHROPIC

Claude Sonnet 5.5 ships

The faster, production-oriented member of the Claude 5.5 family — same $2 / $10 per 1M tokens pricing, but 30%+ faster output and up to 30% lower cost per completed task because it needs fewer tokens and tool calls. Also now generally available in GitHub Copilot.

The recurring theme: cost per token is becoming less important than cost per successful task.

28 SEP · THE NUMBER OF THE WEEK

Anthropic's IPO economics reveal the cost of the frontier

Reuters reviewed Anthropic's IPO prospectus, which reportedly shows plans involving $518 billion in future cloud, computing and infrastructure obligations. That one number explains the AI infrastructure boom: the frontier-model business increasingly requires models → accelerators → memory → networking → power → data centres → hundreds of billions in capital. Reuters' broader analysis warns the industry now needs enormous productivity gains and new revenue pools to justify this expansion economically.
