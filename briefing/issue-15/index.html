01 · AI RESEARCH & DEVELOPMENT

Capability-specific governance arrives.

17 NOV · OPENAI

Frontier AI crosses another cybersecurity threshold

OpenAI's safety evaluation classifies both GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol at its Critical cybersecurity capability threshold, with Astra also reaching the High threshold for AI self-improvement. OpenAI consequently tightened restrictions around higher-risk cyber assistance and now requires additional vetting for more permissive access.

Why it matters: frontier-model governance is becoming capability-specific — general model access → restricted cyber capabilities → verified professionals → monitoring & sandboxing. That is materially different from generic content moderation.

Read OpenAI’s deployment safety report →
20 NOV · OPENAI

MedChemBench tests AI as a medicinal chemist

A new benchmark of 99 problems drawn from real medicinal-chemistry programs: can models interpret molecular structures, connect experimental evidence to results, select compounds and predict properties? GPT-6.2 Astra achieved 84.5% overall credit in OpenAI's evaluation — though compound selection and numeric property prediction remain weaker areas. ExplainX surfaced this as a major science story; its figures were cross-checked against OpenAI's release.

Why it matters: AI-for-science is moving from read papers → summarize science toward interpret experimental evidence → recommend what scientists should test next.

Read OpenAI’s announcement →
16 NOV · OPENAI

Academic-research ambition doubles — toward 200,000 scientists

OpenAI expanded its Academic Researchers program toward 200,000 scientists worldwide, after an initial pilot of 20,000 researchers across 33 countries. Surveyed participants estimated comparable work without AI would have taken nearly twice as long — self-reported productivity data, not a controlled study.

The signal to watch: research throughput per scientist, not simply scientific benchmark scores.

02 · AI MODELS & TOOLS

Cost per completed task is the new frontier.

🥇 17 NOV · OPENAI

GPT-6.2 Astra + Sol launch

The week's biggest model release. Astra targets difficult professional work, scientific research, coding, computer use and long-running agent workflows — new internal highs on several professional and science evaluations while requiring fewer output tokens than GPT-6 Astra, at $8/M input, $40/M output. Sol is the everyday high-performance model for agentic workflows and coding at $2/M input, $10/M output — within 0.5 points of Astra on FrontierCode at roughly one-fifth the cost. OpenAI also introduced discounted 2-hour and 8-hour background execution windows (30% and 40% off token rates) for workflows that don't need immediate responses.

The theme we've tracked for months: model intelligence alone matters less than cost per successfully completed task.

Read OpenAI’s announcement →
🔴 18 NOV · GOOGLE

Gemini 4 Neon targets long-running agent work

Built for software engineering, analytical work, science and long-running agent tasks: a 1-million-token context window at $3/M input, $15/M output, ranking #3 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its highest reasoning setting, per Google. Notably, Google emphasizes steerability — whether users can redirect an agent midway through a task without it losing the objective.

That last point matters. Enterprise-agent quality increasingly means: can it start correctly, recover from mistakes, be redirected, and finish? — not merely "what is its benchmark score?"

Read Google’s announcement →
🟦 17 NOV · MICROSOFT

Ignite turns enterprise AI into an operating layer

Microsoft Ignite introduced a much broader agent architecture: Copilot Workspaces, Microsoft IQ, Agent 365, new MAI models and expanded role-specific copilots for sales, legal, finance and service — plus Code 2 / Code 2 Flash for coding and Cyber 2 Flash for security. Agent 365 acts as a governance and control layer for enterprise agents, and Microsoft says it already manages more than 50 million agents.

For enterprise AI this may matter more than another chatbot: agent → identity → enterprise context → permissions → security → FinOps → outcome measurement.

04 · ⚛️ QUANTUM — WEEKLY UPDATE

Quantum joins the supercomputer.

🥇 16 NOV · IBM × NVIDIA

Quantum integrates with classical supercomputing

IBM and NVIDIA announced deeper integration between quantum computers and HPC systems — including connecting NVIDIA's quantum-resource management into Slurm, the workload scheduler used across the world's supercomputers, so classical clusters can schedule quantum resources natively. With Fermilab, they are also launching a modular quantum-computing co-design platform for high-energy and nuclear physics. Separately, NVIDIA demonstrated a CUDA-Q error-correction decoding technique it says is up to 13,000× faster than the comparison approach in its research.

The architecture increasingly looks like: CPU + GPU + HPC scheduler + QPU + quantum error correction — not a quantum computer functioning by itself.

Read IBM’s SC26 post →
18 NOV · NIST

Quantum physics can verify physical location

NIST researchers demonstrated the first complete experimental quantum position-verification protocol: using entangled photons and a loophole-free Bell test, they could verify that a person or device was genuinely located where it claimed to be. Potential applications span financial transactions, secure buildings, critical infrastructure and anti-phishing authentication — though NIST cautions practical deployment may be a decade or more away.

For BFSI, the concept is fascinating: authentication could eventually prove not only who you are, but physically where you are.

Read NIST’s announcement →
🚨 19 NOV · IOSCO

Global securities regulators: prepare for quantum now

IOSCO — representing securities regulators across more than 130 jurisdictions — warned financial institutions to begin preparing immediately for quantum risks. Powerful quantum computers could eventually threaten conventional encryption, with particularly complex implications for cryptocurrencies and blockchain infrastructure; IOSCO urged firms to begin migration planning now and work with critical technology suppliers rather than waiting for cryptographically relevant quantum computers to arrive.

For financial institutions, the ordering stands: post-quantum cryptography readiness comes before "what quantum application should we build?" The cybersecurity requirement arrives first.

Read the Reuters report →
💰 17 NOV · EARLY-STAGE

Quantum startups attract early-stage capital

Photonic quantum startup Qluster raised €4.7M pre-seed, while Italian superconducting-quantum company Planckian secured a €2.5M European Innovation Council grant, with a further €7.5M equity investment planned subject to due diligence.

Small numbers next to AI financing — but investment keeps spreading across competing quantum architectures: superconducting, photonic, neutral-atom and trapped-ion.
